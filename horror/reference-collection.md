# Reference collection — melting walls, mannequins, and the room that watches back

**Summary (three sentences).**
This is the packet's moodboard in text: an annotated index of the visual references the original brief named, organised by concept rather than by medium, because both effects are symptoms of the same rule. Each entry says what to look at, what it contributes, and what not to copy. No images are stored here — the works are in copyright and most are one search away; the links go to museum, gallery, publisher or developer pages rather than to scraped copies.

## How to use this

- **A** is melting, warping and organic decay in architecture. **B** is mannequins: stillness, multiplicity, the moment the count changes. **C** is the combined idea — a space and figures that both react to attention.
- Each entry gives a **look at** (the specific work or scene), a **why it matters**, and a **takeaway** for Far World. Where a timestamp would normally go, this sheet gives the scene position instead; none of these has a published timecode and the folder does not invent one.
- Mechanics are argued in [moves-when-unseen.md](moves-when-unseen.md), timings in [timed-look-away-reference-sheet.md](timed-look-away-reference-sheet.md), mannequin behaviour in [condemned-barts-behaviour-sheet.md](condemned-barts-behaviour-sheet.md). This sheet is for the eye, not the rule.

## A. Melting walls, warped architecture, organic decay

### A1. Zdzisław Beksiński — the fantastic period (1970s)

**Look at:** the Sanok collection, especially *AC75* (1975), which an exhibition listing describes as a dark sky over several tall flat rock stumps, with a small fire glowing on each ([Berlin.de](https://www.berlin.de/en/tickets/exhibitions/hr-giger-beksiski-ruppert-lynch-in-the-shadow-of-surrealism-37b78f12-b3ed-4257-8f26-a808039ed4b5)). The period as a whole is landscapes and figural compositions that abandon the avant-garde for a personal, solitary imagery ([Muzeum Sanok](https://muzeum.sanok.pl/en/zbiory/zdzislaw-beksinski)).

**Why it matters:** Beksiński's structures do not read as buildings that melted; they read as geology that was once a body. Scale is doing the work — tiny lights against enormous ruined forms — and the palette is bone, ash, umber and black with one warm point.

**Takeaway:** decay should look **structural and slow**, not gooey. A hallway that has sagged two centimetres is more wrong than one running like candle wax. Borrow the scale cue: one small practical light in a large distorted room.

### A2. Francis Bacon — the invisible rooms

**Look at:** the cage-and-ellipse frames around his figures, traced through *Three Studies for Figures at the Base of a Crucifixion* (c. 1944, Tate), *Man in Blue IV* (1954, Mumok) and *Chimpanzee* (1955, Staatsgalerie Stuttgart). The exhibition *Francis Bacon: Invisible Rooms* treats these as architectural framing devices that isolate the sitter ([PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5537596)).

**Why it matters:** the room is not a backdrop; it is a device that presses on the figure. Bacon's interiors are thin, flat planes — a floor line, a doorframe, a rail — and the figure is blurred or smeared against them. Adrian Lyne cited exactly this for *Jacob's Ladder*: "tortured, blurred shots, red streaks and sharp pieces which, when you freeze frame this stuff, looks just like Bacon's drawings" ([American Cinematographer](https://theasc.com/article/jacobs-ladder-cinematography)).

**Takeaway:** define space with **few, hard edges** — skirting, doorframe, picture rail — then distort only what is inside them. The frame stays true; the contents fail. That is also the readability rule for the game: keep the architecture's edges legible while the surface misbehaves.

### A3. H. R. Giger — biomechanical architecture

**Look at:** *Necronom IV* (1976, H. R. Giger Museum, Gruyères) and the *Necronomicon* plates, especially the "biomechanical landscape" interiors where ribs become vaults and machinery becomes tissue ([artincontext](https://artincontext.org/necronom-iv-by-h-r-giger/); [museum biography](https://www.hrgigermuseum.com/hr-giger)). Contemporary coverage describes his palette as monochrome — black and grey, sometimes a single red or green — and his forms as "rusting machinery that suggests diseased tissue" ([Airbrush Action, 1987](https://www.littlegiger.com/articles/files/AirbrushAction_01_1987.pdf)).

**Why it matters:** Giger is the clearest case of architecture and body sharing one material language. Critics have noted the cathedral-like ambition in his monstrous interiors — exaltation and majesty, not just disgust ([Admind](https://admindagency.com/hr-giger-necronomicon)).

**Takeaway:** use Giger as a **material** reference, not a monster reference. Wall surfaces that read as ribbed, wet, or calcified; structure that looks grown rather than built. Keep it monochrome and let one accent colour carry the threat.

### A4. Silent Hill — the Otherworld

**Look at:** the transition from fog to rust. In the first game, fog gives way to inky shadow, rusted chain-link, derelict hospital equipment, body bags and congealed blood ([Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/444219/silent-hill-alessas-anguish-made-manifest-nightmares-in-the-fog)). Chris Pruett's design analysis describes an industrial reality where "night persists indefinitely and rusted metal and barbed wire are core components of most architecture," with geometry that reflects the real building only loosely and confusingly ([Chris's Survival Horror Quest](https://horror.dreamdawn.com/?p=29211)). *Silent Hill: The Terror Engine* adds the surface detail: walls turn to rusty plates and fences or are padded like a madhouse, paint falls from them, and in *Silent Hill 3* walls and floors bleed, swarm and burn like embers in real time ([OAPEN PDF](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12657/24016/1006117.pdf)). The *SH3* team described overlaying a second texture layer like capillaries under skin ([IGN interview, 2002](https://www.ign.com/articles/2002/06/12/silent-hill-3-interview)).

**Why it matters:** the Otherworld is the canonical "the room is a body" look, and it escalates by **material substitution**, not by geometry alone: plaster becomes rust, rust becomes membrane.

**Takeaway:** borrow the material logic — one surface at a time turning wet, rusted or veined — not the blood-and-barbed-wire costume. And note the trigger: the Otherworld is story- and location-gated, not attention-gated. Far World's version has to be quieter and tied to the player's gaze.

### A5. Annihilation (2018) — the Shimmer and the lighthouse chamber

**Look at:** the Shimmer's surface and the chamber under the lighthouse. VFX supervisor Andrew Whitehurst describes the Shimmer as the screenplay's "heat haze, but with prismatic effects, and distorting the background but having a surface quality," balanced between refraction, petrol slick and iridescent car paint; the chamber's geometry was built from Mandelbulb fractals ([vfxblog](https://vfxblog.com/2018/03/12/mandelbulbs-mutations-and-motion-capture-the-visual-effects-of-annihilation)).

**Why it matters:** this is distortion as **refraction and biology**, not as melting wax. It is beautiful before it is threatening, which is why it stays with people.

**Takeaway:** the wrongness can be gorgeous. A wall that refracts the room behind it, or a doorway whose edges are slightly out of register, will read as wrong without any goo. Save the organic horror for the surfaces the light actually touches.

### A6. Jacob's Ladder (1990) — the hospital descent

**Look at:** the ride to the X-ray room and the hospital corridors, where the film uses blurred figures, red streaks and sharp fragments; Lyne's Bacon reference is quoted above. The hospital's lower floors are populated with tortured souls ([American Cinematographer](https://theasc.com/article/jacobs-ladder-cinematography)). *Silent Hill 3*'s subway and hospital are explicitly compared to it ([Project MUSE chapter](https://muse.jhu.edu/pub/166/oa_monograph/chapter/393873)).

**Why it matters:** the horror is **subjective camera** — the space is not melting, the protagonist is. Shake, blur and colour bleed do the work.

**Takeaway:** reserve one or two moments for full subjective distortion ("the player's perception slipped"), and keep the baseline world sharp. If everything wobbles, nothing does.

### A7. Layers of Fear (2016) — the house that edits itself

**Look at:** any door you reopen, and the corridor painted "don't look back." Bloober's design lead explains that a door may now lead to an elongated hallway, stairs or a brick wall, and that surroundings begin to change "the moment the player turns their back" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/terrifying-players-with-unstable-level-design-in-i-layers-of-fear-i-)). PC Gamer's review names the exact effect wanted here: "paintings melt and warp and laugh when you're not looking" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/layers-of-fear-review)).

**Why it matters:** it is the closest shipped implementation of the space half of the concept, and it proves the trick works without a monster.

**Takeaway:** also a warning. PC Gamer's complaint is that it happens in almost every room, so the scares lose impact. Far World should do it **once or twice per wing**, with a stable room between.

## B. Mannequins — stillness, numbers, the count changing

### B1. Tourist Trap (1979)

**Look at:** the waxworks reveal in the third act. The figures are preserved victims, and the film refuses to explain them ([NightTide](https://nighttidemag.com/2025/05/12/sinister-screen-dolls-dummies-and-the-death-of-trust)); the jerky, hand-operated motion is what sells them as alive ([SYFY](https://www.syfy.com/hidden-horrors-of-peacock-tourist-trap)).

**Takeaway:** one wrong joint is enough. Do not animate the whole body smoothly.

### B2. The Twilight Zone — "The After Hours" (1960)

**Look at:** the deserted department-store floor and the mannequin that is exactly someone the protagonist met. Almost no music; the reveal is a close-up and a count ([The View from the Junkyard](https://junkyard.blog/2022/08/05/the-twilight-zone-the-after-hours); [Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3387022/modern-horror-filmmakers-can-learn-twilight-zone)).

**Takeaway:** the scare can be entirely **social** — the figure knows you — with no gore and no chase.

### B3. Silent Hill 2 — the Mannequins

**Look at:** any mannequin encounter. They are headless, armless sewing dummies, two sets of legs joined at a torso, in the manner of Hans Bellmer's dolls; they "stand still in plain sight" and spring when the player is three to five feet away ([StrategyWiki, citing *Book of Lost Memories*](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Silent_Hill/Monsters)).

**Takeaway:** a second species of figure — **proximity-triggered**, not gaze-locked. It makes staring an incomplete strategy, which is exactly what the house needs.

### B4. Condemned: Criminal Origins — Bart's Department Store

**Look at:** the display floor, where enemies pose among mannequins and some figures turn to look; a group follows and multiplies ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/features/what-makes-monster-horror); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/best-mannequin-scares-horror-games)). The scripted-versus-systemic split is in [condemned-barts-behaviour-sheet.md](condemned-barts-behaviour-sheet.md).

**Takeaway:** ambiguity is the product. The player should waste attention on furniture.

### B5. Resident Evil 7 demo — the attic trio

**Look at:** roughly ten minutes into *Beginning Hour*. Three armless mannequins face a wall; after the player turns away and returns, all three face the player, and a fourth stands at the top of the stairs. No stinger, no camera move ([WhatCulture](https://whatculture.com/gaming/resident-evil-10-terrifying-secrets-amp-easter-eggs-you-need-to-see?page=5); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/best-mannequin-scares-horror-games)).

**Takeaway:** the count change is the whole scare. Three becomes four, and the fourth is between you and the exit.

### B6. Resident Evil Village: Shadows of Rose — House Beneviento

**Look at:** the Study door after the fuse goes missing, and the return leg. The Mia mannequin freezes under gaze and advances when unobserved; more join until one blocks the Study door and must be baited aside ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/resident-evil-village-shadows-of-rose-house-walkthrough-puzzles-guide); [Game8](https://game8.co/games/resident-evil-village/archives/523539)). Footsteps and squeaking joints are the evidence of movement ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/resident-evil-village-baby-scene-scary-successor-shadows-of-rose)).

**Takeaway:** the best commercial model for **multiplicity plus audio**. Note the interval and repeat count, not the metres — see the reference sheet.

### B7. Ralph Eugene Meatyard — *Dolls & Masks*

**Look at:** the Fraenkel Gallery portfolio: a headless mannequin on the ground beside a small doll, an eyeless doll on a bed seen through a metal frame, masks and doll heads on pedestals in an overgrown garden. Staged in abandoned domestic spaces ([Fraenkel Gallery](https://fraenkelgallery.com/portfolios/ralph-eugene-meatyard-dolls-masks); [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://www.philamuseum.org/exhibitions/ralph-eugene-meatyard-dolls-and-masks)).

**Why it matters:** photography supplies what games rarely do — **domestic scale**. The figures are the size of furniture and are photographed like family.

**Takeaway:** place figures where a family photograph would place a person, and let the composition, not the pose, be wrong. Black-and-white is a useful check: if it still reads as wrong without colour, the staging works.

## C. Combined — the space and the figures share one rule

No shipped reference does both halves with a single attention rule; that gap is the project.

- **Layers of Fear** is the space half, and it changes the room behind the player. Its weakness is frequency.
- **RE7 / Shadows of Rose** are the figure half, and they change the pose or the count.
- **Condemned** makes figures indistinguishable from set dressing without the room moving.
- **I'm on Observation Duty** makes the player the detector: the scare is that something changed in the room you were not watching, and you have to notice from memory ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1046820/Im_on_Observation_Duty/)).
- **SCP-173 / "Blink"** supply the rule (gaze locks, a break licenses movement) but not the look. See [moves-when-unseen.md](moves-when-unseen.md).
- **Silent Hill 3's storefront** supplies the confirmation beat: the room changed off-screen without the player causing it, and the change is discovered on return ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/best-mannequin-scares-horror-games)).

**The design sentence this collection argues for:** the room and the figures are the same kind of object. Both are furniture until attention leaves them, and both may be different when attention returns.

## E. What this collection deliberately does not do

- **No images.** Copyright, and the packet stays text-only by design. The links are to rights-holder or institutional pages.
- **No invented timecodes.** Scene positions are given where the folder has not measured the footage.
- **No ranking beyond relevance.** "Must-play" here means "necessary to understand the argument," not "best."
