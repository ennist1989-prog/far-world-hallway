# Mannequin Horror

**Summary (three sentences).**
Mannequin horror lands because a mannequin is a human shape with the person subtracted — our proportions, our posture, our clothing, but no breath, no micro-movement, no gaze — so the brain files it as a corpse that is still standing. The scare is usually built on three beats: noticing the figure, doubting your memory of where it was a moment ago, and then seeing it move, which is why games often do it better than films, because the player is the one who chose to look away. What makes it stick is objectification running in both directions, since the mannequin reduces a person to decoration and the real threat is the possibility that you are the one being displayed.

## Top three

1. **Tourist Trap (1979)** — The definitive mannequin film, where wax figures are preserved victims and the killer's telekinesis makes the whole roadside attraction feel like it is breathing.
2. **The Twilight Zone: "The After Hours" (1960)** — The origin of the department-store mannequin scare, using a deserted floor, a figure that is exactly someone you met, and almost no music to make the reveal unbearable.
3. **Condemned: Criminal Origins (2005)** — The best game version, because the mannequins move when you are not looking, some of the figures are simply enemies standing very still, and the ambiguity makes you waste ammunition on furniture.
