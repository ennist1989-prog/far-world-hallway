# Outlast

**Summary (three sentences).**
Outlast builds tension by removing combat entirely: Miles has a camcorder, a stamina bar and lockers, so every encounter resolves into spotting, sprinting and hiding rather than fighting. The camcorder is the masterstroke — its night-vision mode reveals pitch-black corridors but burns batteries, so sight itself becomes a rationed resource, and the green-grey filter makes a familiar asylum look clinical and wrong. Chase sequences are the actual core loop, with enemies that roam, search and reopen hiding spots, doors that take a beat too long to slam, and item pickups that reach out slowly while something is running at you.

## Top three

1. **Outlast (2013)** — The purest expression of chase horror: no weapon, no counter, only the run-hide-run rhythm and a camera that makes seeing cost something.
2. **Alien: Isolation (2014)** — Takes the same powerless-stealth premise and adds an unpredictable stalker with its own senses, teaching players that hiding well is a skill rather than a button.
3. **Clock Tower (1995)** — The point-and-click ancestor of the whole format, where the scare is a pursuer you can only evade and the interface itself is deliberately clumsy under pressure.
