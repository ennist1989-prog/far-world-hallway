# Resident Evil 7: Biohazard

**Summary (three sentences).**
Resident Evil 7 works by shrinking the series to one lived-in Louisiana property and putting the camera inside Ethan's head, where a narrow field of view turns every doorway into a blind spot and every shadow into a possible presence. Its design leans on scarce ammunition, slow reloads, a flashlight that carves a cone rather than lighting a room, locked doors that force backtracking through corridors you have already learned, and safe rooms that function as islands of relief. The domestic dread is the real engine: the Bakers read as a recognisable mother, father and son at a dinner table, the house is cluttered with family photographs and a basement morgue, and the horror comes from a home behaving like a predator rather than a monster behaving like a monster.

## Top three

1. **Resident Evil 7: Biohazard (2017)** — The high-water mark for first-person domestic horror, because it makes an ordinary house the antagonist and gives you just enough gun to feel responsible for your own failures.
2. **P.T. (2014)** — A playable teaser that proved a looping domestic corridor, a locked field of view and one repeating room can be more frightening than any monster reveal.
3. **Layers of Fear (2016)** — A first-person haunted-house game built entirely on the idea that the house itself is rearranging behind your back, which is the domestic-dread premise stripped to its skeleton.
