# Escape the Backrooms and Inside the Backrooms

**Summary (three sentences).**
Both games work because the Backrooms are architecture without purpose — yellow wallpaper, humming fluorescents, damp carpet and endless right angles that read as an office you have never worked in — so the setting supplies dread before any monster appears. Exploration pacing is the real horror system: Escape the Backrooms spreads thirty-plus levels across long route-finding and resource management (flashlights, almond water for a draining sanity meter), while Inside the Backrooms compresses the same feeling into tighter, puzzle-room-driven spaces with randomized solutions that force a group to split up and report back. Co-op makes the liminality bearable and therefore sharper, because proximity voice chat means someone has to talk, someone has to be the one who gets lost, and rules like teammates no-clipping into other areas or needing every player at the exit turn a lonely aesthetic into social dependency.

## Top three

1. **Escape the Backrooms (2022–2025)** — The encyclopaedic version, treating the lore as a museum of liminal levels and using co-op scale (four players, proximity chat, entities that hear you) to make each room a group problem.
2. **Inside the Backrooms (2022)** — The tighter, puzzle-first take, where randomized solutions and cramped spaces make communication the difference between a clean run and a panic.
3. **The Backrooms (Found Footage) (2022–)** — Kane Parsons' short films established the visual grammar these games borrow, proving that a camcorder, a hum and an endless yellow corridor are enough on their own.
