# Amnesia: The Dark Descent

**Summary (three sentences).**
Amnesia: The Dark Descent works because it makes darkness itself the enemy: Daniel's sanity drains in shadow, and the only reliable hiding places are the same shadows that unmake him, so refuge and danger occupy the same space. Its key mechanics are a two-track health and sanity system, finite tinderboxes and lamp oil, monsters that cannot be fought and must be evaded, and hands-on interaction — shoving doors shut, dragging barricades, peering around frames — that makes panic physical. The atmosphere comes from restraint: almost nothing is shown, sound carries most of the threat, and low sanity distorts the screen with swaying vision, breathing and insects while the castle's long corridors do the rest.

## Top three

1. **Amnesia: The Dark Descent (2010)** — The reference implementation of defenseless first-person horror, where the resource you manage is not ammunition but your own mind.
2. **Penumbra: Black Plague (2008)** — Frictional's earlier proof that physics-based interaction and an unkillable pursuer can carry a whole game without a gun.
3. **SOMA (2015)** — Same studio, same helplessness, but sanity pressure is swapped for existential dread and unkillable proxies that make running the only verb that matters.
